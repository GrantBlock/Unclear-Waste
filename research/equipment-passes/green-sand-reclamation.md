# Green Sand Reclamation — Specialty Equipment Pass — Pass date: 2026-08-28

Tracker line items #16 "Sand reclamation" and #17 "Reclaimed Sand Elevator" — both already
sourced to Simpson. As with the mulling pass, this is a primer plus a fit check, not a vendor
search — the open question is which Simpson reclamation method is on order, not who supplies it.

## What reclamation is solving

After shakeout, spent green sand still has good silica in it but is loaded with spent bentonite,
sea coal, and fines from the poured mold. Reclamation strips that off so the sand can go back
into the mulling loop instead of being landfilled — the same sand-cost and disposal-cost problem
that makes mulling efficiency matter also makes reclamation efficiency matter, since both sit on
the same closed loop this line is being built around.

## Two methods, different cost profiles

| Method | How it works | Cost/complexity | Fit for green sand aluminum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanical (pneumatic scrubbing) — Simpson Pro-Claim | Sand is scrubbed against itself pneumatically to strip the clay/binder coating off each grain | Compact footprint, runs continuously without an operator, no post-processing (screening/cooling) needed | Simpson positions this as sufficient for most green sand reclamation on its own — it's the lower-cost, lower-complexity option |
| Thermal (with mechanical pre/post stages) | Three-stage: pneumatic scrub → high-heat burn-off of organics → second pneumatic scrub for final cleaning | Costlier, more process steps, more equipment to maintain | Typically reserved for sand with heavier organic/chemical binder contamination (more common with chemically-bonded core sand mixed into the reclaim stream) than for straight green sand |

For a pure green sand aluminum line without significant chemically-bonded core sand entering the
reclaim stream, mechanical-only (Pro-Claim) is generally the right-sized answer — thermal adds
cost mainly to strip contamination this process may not be generating much of.

## Recommendation for next step

Confirm which configuration is actually on order for line #16 — Pro-Claim mechanical-only, or a
three-stage system with a thermal step. If the line will use cores (chemically bonded, not green
sand) that end up mixed into the shakeout stream, thermal capacity may be worth keeping; if it's
staying core-light, mechanical-only is likely both sufficient and the better cost fit. This also
determines what #17's Reclaimed Sand Elevator needs to interface with (a single Pro-Claim
discharge vs. a three-stage system's output).
